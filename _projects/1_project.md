I am developing stimulation algorithms for brain-computer interfaces (BCIs) that target the cerebellum. The central challenge is learning how neural activity across multiple brain regions encodes motor commands and predictions, and using that knowledge to design real-time adaptive control policies.

**Approach:** I use neural recordings from cerebellar and motor regions to train predictive dynamical models of brain state. These models serve as the basis for adaptive control policies that can respond to neural activity in real time — closing the loop between observation and stimulation.

**Long-term goal:** Support cerebellar prostheses that can restore or augment motor function in patients with cerebellar ataxia or other movement disorders.

**Methods:** Adaptive control, state-space modeling, machine learning, high-dimensional statistics, computational neuroscience.

*This work is ongoing at the [Neural Signals Lab](https://www.bme.jhu.edu/ascharles/) at Johns Hopkins University.*
