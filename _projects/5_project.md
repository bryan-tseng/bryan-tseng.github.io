Electrodermal activity (EDA) is a sensitive measure of autonomic nervous system function, useful for tracking stress, pain, and arousal. However, EDA recordings in clinical environments are often corrupted by motion artifacts and other noise, limiting their utility.

**Approach:** We developed an unsupervised, automated pipeline for detecting and removing artifacts from EDA signals without requiring labeled training data or manual inspection. The method is designed to be robust across the noisy, uncontrolled conditions typical of real clinical deployments.

**Impact:** Clean EDA signals enable downstream analyses — such as nociception monitoring and anesthesia depth tracking — that depend on reliable autonomic signal quality.

*Published in Physiological Measurement (2022). Work conducted at the Neural Statistics Research Laboratory, MIT Picower Institute.*
